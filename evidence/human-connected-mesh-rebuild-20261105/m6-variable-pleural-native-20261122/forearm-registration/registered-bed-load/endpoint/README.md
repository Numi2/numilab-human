# Bed contact at the accepted body pose

The native material check now reads the candidate skeleton after joint integration. The existing compensated articulated forward-kinematics pass refreshes its poses before the stand transaction decides whether to accept the step. The same registered skin weights, measured clearance and two-layer constitutive law drive the contact force and bound its endpoint. An unsupported point sets the existing contact failure status on the GPU. The scene publishes the eight-step prefix and rejects the ninth step.

The matched eight-step trial has identical poses for all 157 bodies, identical contents of four cardiac and thoracic geometry buffers, and byte-identical coupled, support-impulse and support-selection traces. Independent Float64 reconstruction checks 49,294 material columns at 16 ms. Three columns exceed their admissible compression on the 18 ms candidate. At point 17764 the GPU measures 1.762514 mm against 1.430584 mm allowed. The independent evaluation of the failed pose measures 1.762527 mm. The native failure receipt retains the respiratory controller at 16,000 µs while recording its trial at 18,000 µs. This short test verifies the acceptance boundary.

## Physical support capacity

The three points lie above the ascending part of the right trapezius. At the measured opening pose, projected height differences between the skin used for contact and the registered unloaded outer tissue surface are 2.07–2.73 mm there. Across the positive-area footprint, the height difference exceeds the local permitted column shortening on 46.0% of the quadrature area. A vertical ray samples projected surfaces and can intersect different sides of a fold. Only 34,736 of the 49,294 positions have all three boundary heights in order. This comparison cannot serve as a direct skin-to-volume material transfer.

For a fixed initial orientation and straight descent on the rigid bed, a second calculation clips every physical outer skin triangle to the possible contact half-space. It assigns the entire projected area the maximum allowable local material pressure, even counting overlaps. One of the three independently registered source columns limits descent to 3.725 mm. Even that optimistic normal force is only 13.05 N against 952.86 N of body weight. At least 54.51 mm of descent would be needed to expose enough projected area at the same pressure ceiling. These numbers apply to this fixed posture and this column law. Articulated settling, shear and lateral transmission through deeper tissue, and bed deformation require their own mechanics.

The full-volume solver already uses its outer skin for bed contact. Its separate matched test rejected a connected tissue fit earlier than the control. The local contact mode remains a diagnostic until it reproduces the physical surface and transfers bed reaction to the skeleton in a supported pose. The reduced model's earlier long trajectories remain available for cardiopulmonary and geometry regression.
