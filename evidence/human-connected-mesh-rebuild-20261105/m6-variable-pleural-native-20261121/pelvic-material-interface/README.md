# Bilateral gluteal material and load transfer

The native entrance geometry assigns 10.06 mL to both gluteal muscle and sacrum. The two femora and os coxae introduce additional overlap. Independently moving a muscle vertex or increasing a collision tolerance leaves that material double-occupied. This candidate defines separate interior muscle, cortical and deep-fat volumes and uses each original tissue or bone face as the parent of its corresponding interface. This is a registration decision for the supported mixed-source adult, not a measured individual's dissection.

**Volume and contact.** Each registered P1 tetrahedron integrates only its intersection with the muscle. Empty cells contribute zero. All components, including three tiny disconnected remnants per side, participate in the mass and moment accounting. The data retain exact integrals of the nodal shape functions and their pairwise products. Those integrals reproduce the independent polyhedral mass, center of mass and second moment. The minimum carrier shape quality exceeds 6.54e-5 on both sides against the existing 1e-5 gate. Direct fixed-boundary tetrahedralization and connected cavity repairs still left three poor cells. Conforming edge subdivision made that outcome worse. The failed trials are retained.

The 20,285 integration triangles collectively cover every new cortical or adipose interface. The muscle side of a point binds to its four carrier nodes. The opposite side binds to its rigid cortical body and face or three actual registered adipose volume nodes. The native deep boundary matches the oriented topology of all 207,893 D facets and their registered vertex positions. Independently prescribed nonuniform facet pressures reproduced equal and opposite forces, moments, and virtual power with errors below 1e-15 in the corresponding SI units. These manufactured pressures are operator tests. They are not presumed bed or physiological loads. Reintersecting separately rounded Float32 surfaces measured residual overlapping volumes up to 4.17e-6 mL. The binary64 Boolean has signed roundoff at the scale reported in intersections.json.

**Native mass partition.** The full scene's production mass compiler and rigid COM rebasing ran on SSH Numi after the existing tissue debits. A candidate debit of 0.483657 kg from pelvis, 0.126657 kg from right femur and 0.135307 kg from left femur passed with maximum packed relative moment error 4.24e-8. The three donors retain positive residual mass and admissible inertia. Donor proportions are inferred from the existing pelvic and femoral material weights integrated over the cut volume. The solver uses lumped inertia in this preflight, and the difference from consistent distributed inertia is recorded. The check constructs a candidate. The executing scene keeps its original physical mass owner until the new muscle and contact forces can participate together.

**Motion and remaining failure.** A replay of 938 retained accepted poses plus the registered entrance over 60 simulated seconds measured positive passive-material Jacobians throughout. Independent containment rays still found muscle quadrature points inside bone at 64 ms on each side. Across the five audited times the largest inward offsets relative to the originating cortical facet planes were 0.579 mm right and 0.331 mm left. A plane offset is not the shortest distance to the solid boundary. This proves the current donor motion cannot enforce the unilateral interface, even with the initial overlapping material removed. The supported scene has no moving deep-fat FEM operator; it cannot certify deep-fat contact.

The new executable ran the complete full-tissue configuration for eight accepted steps, 0.125 ms in 245.84 s wall time including initialization. The accepted body-state hash, both geometry hashes, and all 77 physiological columns matched the unmodified control. No new muscle force or mass was applied to that run. The previous 60-second result at 0.1044× uses the supported, condensed bed owner. The full deformable model has still to transmit bed loads through the pelvic material and settle. Some cut carriers have exceptionally light positive-mass nodes. Enabling their free dynamics needs conditioning and a true residual check; adding a mass floor would change the body.
