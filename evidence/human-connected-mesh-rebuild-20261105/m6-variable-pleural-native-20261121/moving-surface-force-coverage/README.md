# Moving surface force coverage

The moving facet registry could bind anatomically valid contact to tissue parents outside the original unit-load operator. The two closed gluteal surfaces respectively required 609 and 629 additional material parents. A contact force on an unavailable parent had no compiled response. The static pool and the selected control's first rejecting material face already had their required parents.

The existing motion registry compiler now accepts a material partition and its registered pose receipt. It completes the same constitutive response atlas over the union of the registered faces' nonzero P1 shape supports. It preserves every value in the original columns, anatomical attachments and the existing explicit rigid-mode quotient for disconnected material components. Physical trial acceptance continues to check unprojected tissue and contact forces. The added columns describe responses of the existing physical operator. They do not add fixed points, body mass or external forces.

Rebuild with `python prepare_motion_registry.py PHYSICAL_P1_BOUNDARY NEW_ASSETS --material-partition ROUNDED_PARTITION --registration-receipt SOURCE_POSE_RECEIPT`. Use the resulting directory as the normal material asset argument of the native compiler and launcher. The older two-argument form reconstructs the historical registry. The full compiler entrypoint rebuilt all 47 runtime payloads byte for byte. The previous Float32 atlas was about 50 MiB and the completed atlas is about 117 MiB.

Unit-load solves covered every newly mapped parent before Float32 emission. The maximum double-precision residual was 2.12e-12 N. Independent mixed old/new loads gave relative stiffness-energy norm errors below 5.35e-8. A Metal fixture compared actual newly available cached responses with the independent native regional triangular solve on both sides, including ordinary free parents, attachments and floating-component parents. All six comparisons passed, with maximum relative displacement-vector error of 1.17e-6 and exact zero displacement at both constrained parents.

At 30 accepted steps, the matched reference and complete-atlas runs had byte-identical material state, surfaces, moving registry, contact time series, fresh force certificates and physiological trace. The run with the wider atlas took 22.35 s including startup and approximately 12.79 s in the body owner for 0.060 s of simulated time. The two measurements are single invocations.

The experimental mixed recovery configuration still rejected its 95th proposal with either atlas. The original trial's terminal GPU capture contains 1,016 nonfinite right-side free displacements and no unmapped nonzero-weight parents among the final slots. This experiment separates a real mapping deficiency from the numerical failure. Neither the atlas tests nor the 30-step reference qualify sustained whole-body motion.
