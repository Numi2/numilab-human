# Patellar motion authority and native admission — 2026-09-30

The patella's visible position must be judged against the body frame and the
joint law actually used by the running model. The pinned Open Knee FEBio source
prescribes tibiofemoral flexion, but its three named patellar cylindrical
joints leave rotation and translation unprescribed. Its PTC, QAT, and PTL
regions are tied to the patellar bone. The pinned MyoSim whole-body model,
however, activates three polynomial patellar joint equalities per side, each
driven by knee angle. The two sources therefore do not specify one common
patellar trajectory. The source-bound audit and exact coefficients are in
[`patellar-motion-authority-20260930/receipt.json`](media/patellar-motion-authority-20260930/receipt.json),
regenerated by `tools/audit_patellar_motion_authority.py`.

An opt-in native diagnostic in the Matter visual probe releases exactly the
three MyoSim patellar equalities for the selected knee and seeds those
coordinates from the source-projected neutral pose. It preserves the default
mode and does not claim to implement the three Open Knee cylindrical-joint
constraints or identify a measured patellar motion law. Its native build
completed. With the same source payloads, four left quadriceps routes, a
1 µs timestep, and one requested step, both the baseline and the diagnostic
failed **before completing step zero**. The diagnostic released the expected
three equalities, but both runs were rejected by deformable contact between
native objects 1 and 2. The loaded payload's selected-region order maps these
objects to PCL and ACL. The FEBio source includes `PCL_To_ACL` contact, so
turning that pair off would evade the source contract. Full commands, hashes,
and native logs are recorded in
[`native-diagnostic-receipt.json`](media/patellar-motion-authority-20260930/native-diagnostic-receipt.json).

This is a concrete admission blocker for loaded knee motion. The next
mechanics step is to resolve the PCL–ACL initial contact state without
discarding the source-authored pair, then rerun matched constrained and
unprescribed trajectories with articular clearance, contact force, tendon
reactions, energy balance, and replay/rollback checks. Neither source audit
nor this rejected one-step diagnostic confirms clinically correct patellar
placement or a corrected standing simulation.
